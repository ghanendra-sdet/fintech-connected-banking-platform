# Connected Banking — User & Business Flow

> While [`architecture-and-flow.md`](./architecture-and-flow.md) documents the internal
> onboarding/whitelisting state machine, this document covers the **actual end-to-end business
> flow** — what a business experiences linking and using a bank account, including multi-account
> behavior, consent, and how the real load test (see
> [`test-reports/load-testing-report.md`](../test-reports/load-testing-report.md)) maps onto
> these flows.

## Actors

| Actor | Role in the flow |
|---|---|
| **Business** | The account holder linking and using bank accounts through the platform |
| **Bank** | Holds the actual funds; whitelists the platform's IP; is the source of truth for balance/transactions |
| **Connected Banking Platform** | Orchestrates linking, consent, transaction retrieval, and fee collection |
| **Consuming System (ERP / internal finance tool)** | The business's own system integrating via the platform's API |

---

## 1. End-to-End Business Flow (High Level)

```
Business signs up and completes self-service eKYC
        │
        ▼
Business links a bank account (Bank Account Linking Service)
        │
        ▼
Bank Account Verification Service validates the account is real
        │
        ▼
Business requests bank whitelisting (the one manual, bank-side step)
        │
        ▼
Bank whitelists the platform as a Digital Service Provider
        │
        ▼
Bank Balance Service exposes live balance; Transaction Service exposes history
        │
        ▼
Business (or its ERP, via API) initiates transactions, views Mini Statements, pulls Reports
        │
        ▼
Commercial Lifecycle Service deducts fees from the dedicated Fee Wallet per transaction
        │
        ▼
Reconciliation Service confirms platform records match bank-side records
```

**Testing implication:** this flow has **two independent sources of truth** — the bank's own
records and the platform's local representation of them. Nearly every high-value defect in this
module is a **drift** between those two, not a bug in either one alone.

---

## 2. Multi-Account Linking Business Flow

A business is not limited to one bank account — this is where the platform earns its "Connected"
name.

```
Business has Account A already linked and ACTIVE (whitelisted, balance visible)
        │
        ▼
Business links a second bank Account B
        │
        ▼
Account B goes through its OWN independent Verification → Whitelisting cycle
        │
        ▼
Account A's status, balance, and transaction history must remain completely unaffected
        │
        ▼
Dashboard/Overview must now correctly aggregate BOTH accounts —
   e.g. a combined balance view, or clearly account-scoped views, per product design
```

**Key test scenarios:** linking a second account must never reset, pause, or re-trigger
whitelisting on the first; a transaction search/filter must correctly scope to the account
selected, never silently mixing Account A's and Account B's transactions.

## 3. Transaction & Reporting Business Flow

```
A transaction occurs on the bank side (credit or debit)
        │
        ▼
Transaction Service retrieves/receives the record from the bank
        │
        ├──▶ Credits ──▶ shown in Credits view, included in "money in" totals
        │
        └──▶ Debits ──▶ shown in Debits view, included in "money out" totals
        │
        ▼
Transaction Search Service makes it filterable (by type, date, amount, reference)
        │
        ▼
Mini Statement Service generates a shorter, printable view of recent activity
        │
        ▼
Banking Report / Account Report / Transaction Report Services generate exportable reports
        │
        ▼
Export / Download Services produce the final CSV/PDF file
```

**Key test scenarios:**
- A Credits-only filter must never leak Debit entries, and vice versa
- The Mini Statement is typically generated by a **separate code path** than the main
  Transactions view (optimized for a shorter, printable format) — its totals must reconcile
  exactly against the full Transactions view for the same period
- Every downstream report (Banking, Account, Transaction) must trace back to the same underlying
  transaction data — a mismatch between any two reports for the same date range is a reportable
  defect on its own, even without knowing yet which one is "wrong"

## 4. Consent Management Business Flow

Connected Banking accesses a business's real bank data — this makes consent a first-class flow,
not a background setting.

```
Business grants consent during onboarding (implicit in agreeing to link an account)
        │
        ▼
Consent Management Service marks the account's data-access consent as ACTIVE
        │
        ▼
Transaction Service / Bank Balance Service are only queryable while consent is ACTIVE
        │
        ▼
Business (or admin) revokes consent
        │
        ▼
Consent Management Service marks it REVOKED
        │
        ▼
Transaction Service / Bank Balance Service access must be restricted IMMEDIATELY —
   not on the next scheduled sync, not after a cache expiry
```

**Key test scenario:** consent revocation is a **security-critical** test case, not just a
functional one — any delay between revocation and access being cut off is effectively an
unauthorized data access window.

---

## 5. Fee Wallet Business Flow (Deeper View)

See the README for the high-level fee wallet model. From a business-flow perspective, the
important detail is *when* the fee check happens relative to the actual bank-side transaction:

```
Transaction initiated
        │
        ▼
Commercial Lifecycle Service checks Fee Wallet balance BEFORE submitting to the bank
        │
        ├──▶ Insufficient ──▶ Transaction blocked, bank never contacted, wallet untouched
        │
        ▼
Sufficient ──▶ Transaction submitted to the bank
        │
        ▼
Bank confirms the transaction
        │
        ▼
Fee + GST is deducted from the Fee Wallet — only now, after bank confirmation
```

**Key test scenario:** what happens if the bank-side transaction succeeds but the fee deduction
step fails immediately after (e.g. a platform outage between the two steps)? The transaction
must not be "free," but it also must not silently double-attempt the fee deduction later without
being reconciled against what the bank actually confirmed.

## 6. How the Real Load Test Maps Onto These Flows

The [load testing executive report](../test-reports/load-testing-report.md) — 405,067
transactions, ~80.2 TPS stable, 0.001% error rate — validated specifically the **Transaction
Service path** in section 1 above: initiation → validation → ledger posting → bank adapter
invocation → status polling → final response handling (see the report's Test Scope).

What that means for the flows documented here:

- **Validated under load:** the core transaction submission and status-polling path (sections 1
  and 3 above)
- **Not covered by that load test** (worth flagging as a gap, not assuming it's covered):
  Multi-account linking under concurrent load (section 2), consent revocation latency under load
  (section 4), and Mini Statement/Report generation throughput (section 3's reporting half) —
  these would need their own dedicated load scenarios rather than being assumed safe by
  association with the transaction-path results

This is a good example of a broader QA lesson: a strong load test result for one critical path
does not automatically extend to every adjacent feature — it only covers exactly what was in
scope, which is why the load report's own "Test Scope" section matters as much as its results.

---

## 7. Why This View Matters for Test Design

The onboarding/whitelisting state machine in `architecture-and-flow.md` answers *"is this
account correctly connected?"* — this document answers *"once connected, does everything that
can happen to the data and the money stay correct, consistent, and secure?"* The highest-value
defects here live in cross-source drift (platform vs. bank), cross-account isolation, and
consent timing — exactly the scenarios captured as edge cases in
[`test-cases/regression-checklist.md`](../test-cases/regression-checklist.md).
